## How the program works

Sixteen weeks, three 20–30 minute sessions a week, from Monday 12 October 2026 to Sunday 31 January 2027. It ends with a three-part benchmark that measures shoulder and grip endurance, repeat-sprint capacity and heart-rate recovery.

### Your rope and what it means for progression

Your rope is 8.5 m × 35 mm, about 8.5 kg, so each arm drives roughly 4.2 m and 4.2 kg of rope. That is light and short compared with gym ropes (12–15 m, 12–18 kg). Waves travel fast and load per stroke is modest, so you cannot progress by making the rope heavier. Difficulty comes from the levers below instead.

| Lever | How to apply it | Why it gets harder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Amplitude | Bigger waves, hands rising to chest height | Each stroke lifts more rope mass higher, so more work per stroke |
| Cadence | More strokes per minute (metronome) | Power is work per stroke × strokes per second |
| Density | Longer work, shorter rest | Phosphocreatine needs ~30 s to half-recover; short rests push the load onto glycolysis and the aerobic system |
| Distance to anchor | Step 30–50 cm closer | More slack lies on the floor, so every wave must lift more rope to reach the anchor |
| Stance | Half-kneel, lunge, squat hold | Removes leg drive, or adds an isometric leg load on top |
| Compound moves | Add squats, jumps, burpees, lunges | More muscle mass working means higher oxygen demand |

Standard position for every session and test: step back until the rope is just straight, then step 50 cm towards the anchor. Mark that spot with tape so tests are repeatable. If you outgrow this rope after week 16, a 12 m × 38 mm rope roughly triples the mass per arm.

### The end goal

The goal is a composite benchmark at the week-16 retest (around 29–31 January 2027). Hitting all three means the program worked; hitting two is a good result.

| Test | What it measures | Week-16 target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 Continuous alternating waves at 150 bpm | Shoulder and grip endurance | 3:00, or double your baseline if baseline is already 1:30 or more |
| T2 Tabata double waves, 8 × 20 s on, 10 s off | Repeat-sprint capacity and fade | Total reps +25 %, fade 15 % or less |
| T3 Heart-rate recovery 60 s after T2 | Aerobic recovery | Drop at least 6 bpm larger than baseline |

These thresholds are my calibration of typical gains over 12–16 weeks for someone new to a modality. There are no well-established population norms for battle ropes, so the targets are relative to your own baseline.

### Weekly structure

- **A, Endurance and density.** Longer intervals at controlled intensity. Drives T1 and T3.
- **B, Anaerobic intervals.** Short, near-maximal efforts. Drives T2.
- **C, Power and strength endurance.** Slams, rotational throws and compound moves. Builds hips, trunk and grip so the waves hold form.

Run them on non-consecutive days, for example Monday A, Wednesday B, Friday C. Every fourth week is a deload that ends in a retest. Each session follows the same frame: 6–8 min warm-up, 12–20 min main work, up to 5 min finisher, 3 min cool-down.

### Safety notes

- **Lower back.** Slams and rainbows load the lumbar spine. Hinge at the hips and brace; stop the set when your back starts to round.
- **Elbows and forearms.** A death grip loads the wrist extensor tendons, which is the classic route to lateral elbow pain. Grip only as hard as needed and alternate overhand and neutral grips.
- **Shoulders.** Pain above 3 out of 10, or pain still there the next morning, means swap overhead and slam work for low-amplitude in-out waves and snakes for a week.
- **Heart rate.** Wrist sensors are unreliable here. Arm motion corrupts the optical signal and the sensor can lock onto your stroke cadence instead of your pulse. Use a chest strap for tests.
- **Other sport.** If you climb or do other grip-heavy activities, keep session C at least 48 hours before them.

## Exercise library

Twenty exercises in five patterns. Default stance for all standing moves: feet shoulder-width, quarter squat, hips back, chest up, elbows soft. Any move gets easier with smaller amplitude or a step away from the anchor.

| Code | Exercise | Pattern | Mainly trains | Cue | Make it harder |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AW | Alternating waves | Vertical wave | Front shoulders, forearms, trunk anti-rotation | Arms alternate like drumming; trunk stays square and resists twisting | Lunge stance, step closer, faster cadence |
| DW | Double waves | Vertical wave | Shoulders, lats, hips | Both arms together; small hip extension with each wave | Add a squat every 4th wave |
| HK | Half-kneeling alternating waves | Vertical wave | Shoulders and trunk without leg help | Back knee down, front shin vertical, glute of the down leg squeezed | Narrower knee stance |
| SW | Seated waves, feet raised | Vertical wave | Trunk flexors, shoulders | Lean back ~30°, feet off floor, waves still reach the anchor | Straighten the legs |
| SQ | Squat-hold waves | Vertical wave | Quads isometric plus shoulders | Thighs near parallel, AW or DW on top | Lower squat, longer hold |
| LW | Alternating lunge waves | Vertical wave plus locomotion | Legs, balance, shoulders | Reverse lunge alternating sides while waving continuously | Jump lunges |
| SH | Lateral shuffle waves | Vertical wave plus locomotion | Hips, calves, shoulders | Shuffle 1 m left and right, waves never stop | Wider shuffle |
| JS | Squat jump with double slam | Vertical wave plus jump | Whole-body power | Slam, sink into squat, jump, land soft, repeat | Higher jump |
| IO | In-out waves | Horizontal wave | Chest, rear shoulders | Arms sweep apart and together; ropes clap or separate | Bigger sweep |
| SN | Snakes | Horizontal wave | Shoulders at low stress, forearms | Hands near floor, side-to-side sweeps; good for deload and sore shoulders | Faster sweeps |
| OC | Outside circles | Circle | Rear shoulders, rotator cuff | Each hand draws circles outward; alternate direction each set | Bigger circles |
| IC | Inside circles | Circle | Front shoulders, chest | Circles inward | Bigger circles |
| PS | Power slams | Slam | Lats, posterior chain, trunk flexors | Ropes overhead with rise on toes, then hinge and slam hard | Add a jump before each slam |
| SS | Side slams | Rotational slam | Obliques, hips | Lift ropes over one shoulder, rotate and slam to the opposite side | Pivot the back foot fully |
| GT | Grappler throws | Rotational throw | Hip rotation power, obliques | Ends held at hip, rotate and throw across the body, pivot the back foot | Faster cycle |
| RB | Rainbows | Arc | Shoulders, obliques | Sweep both ropes in an arc over the body from side to side; keep a neutral spine | Wider arc |
| BP | Rope burpee | Slam plus burpee | Whole body, heart rate | One power slam, drop ropes, burpee, pick up, repeat | Add a push-up |
| JJ | Rope jumping jacks | Abduction | Side shoulders, calves | Jumping jack with ropes in hands; arms rise to shoulder height | Arms to overhead |
| SA | Single-arm waves | Unilateral wave | Grip, anti-rotation trunk | One rope only, other hand on hip; switch at half time | Lunge stance |
| FW | Freestyle mix | Any | Coordination, conditioning | Change pattern every 5 s without breaking rhythm | Add locomotion |

### Warm-up for every session, 6 to 8 min

1. Arm circles and arm swings, 60 s.
2. 10 hip hinges and 10 bodyweight squats.
3. Jumping jacks without rope, 60 s.
4. Rope ramp-up at 50–60 % effort: AW 20 s, DW 20 s, IO 20 s, with 20 s rest after each.

### Cool-down, 3 min

Walk for 2 min until breathing settles, then stretch chest, lats and forearm flexors and extensors for 20 s each.

### Logging each session

Record the template and level, session effort (0–10), total minutes, and the rep count of your first Tabata or sprint interval. [Recurrent evaluation and tracking](#recurrent-evaluation-and-tracking) explains how these feed the adjustments.

## Baseline evaluation

Run the baseline on Monday 12 October as the first session of week 1, after one practice session on Saturday 10 October. Results set your starting levels and your week-16 targets.

The practice session matters because early gains on any new movement are mostly skill, not fitness. Testing cold would understate your baseline and inflate your apparent progress. On Saturday, run W1 at L1 and spend 2 × 30 s rehearsing waves to a 150 bpm metronome.

### Setup, identical for every test

- Same anchor (a post or rack upright, not a door), same tape mark, same shoes, similar time of day.
- Metronome app at 150 bpm. One hand strikes per beat, so 75 strokes per arm per minute.
- Phone on video from the side, with you and the anchor in frame.
- Chest-strap heart-rate monitor and a timer.
- No hard training in the 48 hours before.

### Protocol, about 30 min

1. **Warm-up.** Standard warm-up, then 2 × 15 s of AW at 150 bpm. Rest 3 min.
2. **T1, continuous alternating waves.** AW to the 150 bpm metronome until failure. Failure is the first of: waves stop reaching the anchor for 3 strokes, you lose the beat for 3 beats, or you drop a rope. Record the time and what stopped you: shoulders, grip, breathing, or legs and back.
3. **Rest 8 min.** Walk, sip water.
4. **T2, Tabata double waves.** DW at maximum effort, 8 rounds of 20 s on and 10 s off. One rep is one double wave hitting the floor. Count reps per round afterwards from the video, at half speed.
5. **T3, heart-rate recovery.** Note heart rate the moment round 8 ends, stand still and upright, and note it again at 60 s. Recovery is the difference.
6. **Notes.** Session effort 0–10, and any pain 0–10 by body region.

### Calculations

- T2 total is the sum of all 8 rounds.
- T2 fade = (1 − (round 7 + round 8) ÷ (round 1 + round 2)) × 100 %. Lower is better.
- Week-16 targets: T1 3:00, or double the baseline if it is 1:30 or more. T2 total × 1.25 with fade 15 % or less. T3 baseline recovery + 6 bpm.

### How the results set your start

| T1 result | Tier | Starting adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 45 s | 1 | Run the plan exactly as written |
| 45 s to 90 s | 2 | Run every A and B session one level higher than written, capped at L4. Once capped, step 30 cm closer to the anchor instead |
| Over 90 s | 3 | Tier 2 adjustments, plus W2 at 150 bpm from L1 and start 30 cm closer to the anchor |

Two extra rules on top of the tier. If T2 fade is above 30 %, run B sessions one level lower than your tier says, since sprint repeatability is the weak link. If grip was what stopped T1, add the W8 finisher to every A session in block 1.

## 16-week plan

Four 4-week blocks, each building for three weeks and closing with a deload week and a retest. Levels below are for tier 1; apply your tier adjustment from the baseline. Sessions grow from about 20 minutes in block 1 to about 30 minutes in block 4.

The taper works because fitness built over months fades over weeks, while fatigue clears in days. Cutting volume by about half for 5–7 days lets you test at full capacity.

## Recurrent evaluation and tracking

Retest every four weeks with the baseline protocol, unchanged, at the end of each deload week. Between retests, three weekly signals tell you whether to push or back off.

Heart-rate recovery varies by 3–5 bpm from day to day with sleep, heat and hydration. Judge T3 across two consecutive retests, not one.

### Weekly signals

- **Session load.** Effort (0–10) × minutes, summed per week. Keep the weekly rise within about 15 %, except the jump back after a deload. Tendons adapt more slowly than heart and lungs, so load spikes outpace them. The evidence for exact thresholds is weak; treat this as a guardrail, not a law.
- **Marker interval.** Reps in the first 20 s interval of every B session. A drop of more than 10 % across two consecutive B sessions signals accumulated fatigue.
- **Morning resting heart rate.** A 7-day average from your watch; at rest, wrist readings are fine. Three or more days at 5 bpm above your normal means you are under-recovered.

If either of the last two signals fires, run the next session as W9 and resume the plan after it.

### Decision rules after each retest

| Result against the previous test | Action for the next block |
| --- | --- |
| T1 and T2 both improved by 5 % or more | Continue as written |
| T1 flat (within 5 %), T2 improved | Add one interval or one round to every A session |
| T2 flat, T1 improved | Add one interval to every B session |
| Both flat | Repeat the previous block's week-3 levels for one extra week, then continue; the plan extends by a week |
| Either fell by more than 5 %, or fade worsened by 10 points | Check sleep and weekly load, then rerun the block one level lower |
| Pain of 3 or more lingering into the next day | Replace slams and rainbows with snakes and in-out waves for a week; if it lasts two weeks, see a physiotherapist |

### After week 16

If you hit the targets, there are two sensible next steps. Either set new targets from the final benchmark and repeat blocks 3 and 4, or move to a 12 m × 38 mm rope and restart at block 2. To simply hold your level, two sessions a week (one A, one B) is usually enough.
